<Aside type="caution">
These instructions were written for and have only been tested on `phoenix` devices (PICO 4, PICO 4 Pro and PICO 4 Enterprise).
They likely will not work on `sparrow` devices (PICO 4 Ultra or Ultra Enterprise). **If you try anyway, here be dragons!**
</Aside>

<Aside type="danger">
Locking the bootloader, like unlocking it, **requires** your headset to be factory reset! **You will lose anything you do not back up!**
</Aside>

## Before you begin

<Steps>
1. Ensure you have the files output by `picounlock` to the `device_backup` folder (or your own dump of `abl.bin` and optionally `devinfo.bin`), as well as your unlock key.
2. Connect your headset to your computer via a reliable USB-C cable.
3. You will also need a copy of `adb` and `fastboot`. If these are not installed on your computer, they are included with `picounlock`.
</Steps>

## Steps

<Steps>
1. `adb reboot bootloader`
2. `fastboot oem picoXXXXXXXX unlock`, replacing `XXXXXXXX` with your unlock key.
</Steps>
<Aside type="caution">
If you used the original version of `picounlock`, you will need to restore your backed up `devinfo.bin`.
This step was determined to be unnecessary in future releases, and was removed.

`fastboot flash devinfo <path_to_backed_up_devinfo>`
</Aside>
<Steps>
3. `fastboot flash abl <path_to_backed_up_abl>`
4. `fastboot oem setenforce 1`
5. `fastboot flashing lock`
6. `fastboot flashing lock_critical`
7. Boot the headset with `fastboot reboot`. After a short wait, Android Recovery should ask you to factory reset - do so!
</Steps>

Your headset should now be in a stock state, with SELinux `Enforcing` and a locked bootloader!
If this is not the case, proceed to [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).

### Troubleshooting

#### Headset will not get past the bootloader

There are a few reasons for this - you can determine the issue you are encountering via running `adb reboot recovery`.
If the headset **does** reboot into recovery mode, you have likely modified a partition and did not return it to a stock state before locking the bootloader.
A locked bootloader will not accept this - the easiest way recover your headset is to [sideload an OTA image](/guides/ota).

If the headset **does not** boot into recovery mode, it is likely that you did not correctly reflash the stock ABL file.
As long as you are still using the PICO Neo 3 Pro ABL file used to unlock the device, this too is recoverable.
You can determine whether this is the case by how the bootloader screen looks. If you can read `kona`, you are still using the PICO 3 ABL.
